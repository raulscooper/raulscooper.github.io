01
Selection criteria
How to choose a spot
Not every dirty spot is the right spot. Picking strategically makes the difference between a lasting transformation and wasted effort.
The scoring system

Score every candidate spot before committing

Rate each spot 1–5 on four criteria. A total score below 12 means skip it — find a better candidate.

Criterion 1
Visibility
Seen by 50+ people/day? High-footfall spots create the most behaviour change.
Criterion 2
Access
Can volunteers reach it safely on foot? Is there space to work?
Criterion 3
Recurrence
Can future dumping be physically blocked or made harder through design?
Criterion 4
Ownership
Is there someone nearby willing to maintain it after the fix?
Priority spot types

Where to look first

1
Temple, mosque, church entrances — high footfall, community pride attached, easier to mobilise locals
2
School gates — children are the future maintainers; the school can formally own upkeep
3
Market corners and nala edges — chronic dump spots that create visible, dramatic transformations
4
Bus stop surrounds — captive audience witnesses the before and after; high symbolic value
Avoid these for now
Active construction zones Private land without consent Industrial or chemical waste No local owner available Politically contested locations

04
Critical logistics
Disposal
The biggest operational failure of cleanup drives is generating bags of garbage with nowhere to go. Solve this before the event — never on the day.

Non-negotiable: Disposal must be arranged at least 5 days before the event. A spotfix that leaves bags piled on the street has made things worse, not better.

Pre-event checklist
1
Contact the ward sanitation inspector directly. Request a pickup vehicle at the end of the event. Get a named person's number.
2
Pre-sort on-site — wet/organic in green bags, dry recyclables in blue. Many cities refuse unsegregated waste.
3
Locate the nearest kabadiwala in advance — plastic, metal, cardboard. Often collected free or for a small payment.
4
Identify a composting facility for organic waste — vermicompost units, municipal sites, or a community garden.
Hazardous material protocol

Do not touch these items

Syringes, medical waste, chemicals, unknown liquids, asbestos — flag with tape, photograph with GPS, submit separately to the municipality's hazardous waste cell. Never bag with general waste.

05
Money
Funding a spot
Money must never concentrate at the centre. Each spot is funded independently. This prevents corruption, capture, and the NGO disease of funding-seeking replacing mission.
“Fund the spot, not the organisation.”
Funding tiers — simplest first
1
Self-funded team — 12 volunteers contributing ₹200–350 each covers a basic clean. Zero bureaucracy.
2
Local business contribution — ask nearby shops to donate materials, not cash. Simpler and corruption-proof.
3
RWA or housing society funds — request ₹5,000–10,000 from the society's discretionary maintenance budget.
4
Platform micro-crowdfunding — any nominated spot can receive public donations capped at ₹25,000. Funds released against receipts only.
5
Corporate CSR top-up — for complex spots only. Subject to strict no-branding rules (see Module 7).

All spot-level spending is photographed, receipted, and posted publicly within 7 days. No exceptions. Transparency is the accountability mechanism.

06
Official partnership
Working with municipalities
Don't ask for money or permission. Ask for three specific, low-cost commitments that make the clean durable.
The Spot Adoption Agreement

A one-page social contract

Not a legal document. Signed by a named ward sanitation inspector and the spot's local owner.

M
Municipality commits to garbage pickup minimum 3× per week, and to installing one waste bin within 30 days
O
Local owner commits to a monthly walk-through and reporting deterioration via the platform within 48 hours
S
SpotClean commits to a 90-day follow-up visit with a public condition report posted online
How to get a yes
1
Don't approach the top. Go to the ward sanitation inspector — they control trucks and schedules with fewer political pressures.
2
Frame it as free labour for them. The ask is minimal — one truck visit, one bin within 30 days.
3
Show them the platform score. Ward scores are tied to state rankings. A documented clean spot improves their metrics.
4
Name them on the platform. Credit when things go well, gentle accountability when they don't.
07
Corporate participation
How corporations can help
Corporations are fuel, not drivers. Funders never determine which spots get cleaned, how they are cleaned, or what is written on the walls.
What corporations can do
Fund materials for platform-nominated spots Provide employees as volunteers Fund platform operating costs (disclosed publicly) Receive internal ESG and CSR recognition Count volunteer hours toward CSR requirements
What corporations cannot do
No logo on cleaned walls or surfaces No company name on any installed signage No selecting which spots receive funding No press releases naming specific spots No employees-only events — must mix with community No naming rights of any kind

Any corporate that cannot accept these terms is not the right partner. The no-branding rule is the most important safeguard against the model becoming an advertising platform.

The pitch in plain language

Your employees spend one Saturday morning cleaning a real spot alongside local residents — no speeches, no banners. You get ESG documentation and team-building value. We get materials and capable volunteers. That's the whole deal.

08
Behaviour change
Messaging after a clean
Shame-based messaging doesn't work. “Do not litter — fine ₹500” is ignored everywhere. What works is signalling that the space is cared for and belongs to someone.
Physical interventions that change behaviour
1
Anti-litter texture coat on walls — rough-textured paint discourages urination and poster-plastering. The single most proven physical intervention.
2
A mural or geometric pattern — even a simple design signals intentionality. People don't litter painted walls as readily as blank ones.
3
A living plant, visibly tended — nothing signals ongoing human care more powerfully than a living thing.
4
A cemented, named bin — anchored, clearly marked, regularly emptied. Its presence alone changes disposal behaviour.
The one message to leave behind
“This space is loved by the people of [neighbourhood name]. Help us keep it this way.”

In the local language. On a durable tile or painted text. No organisation name on the wall. QR code on a small steel plate bolted nearby — links to the spot's public page.

What not to write
Do not litter — fine ₹500 CCTV surveillance in progress Keep India clean Any organisation name or logo Government scheme branding
09
Structure
Keeping it lean
Every Indian civic organisation eventually gets captured — by a politician, a funder, or its own bureaucracy. This structure is designed to make capture structurally difficult.
Organisational structure
1
No national president, no national office. The methodology is the organisation. Anyone following this playbook is SpotClean. No one can be expelled or installed.
2
A 5-person platform trust — the only formal entity. Rotating 2-year terms, publicly named, financials open in real time. One term only.
3
City nodes, not chapters. Loose groups of active spot owners per city. No elections, no hierarchy. One node cannot make decisions for another.
4
All money is spot-level. Central fund maximum: ₹10 lakh. Any donation above ₹1 lakh disclosed publicly within 48 hours.
5
One paid contractor. A platform manager on an annual contract, renewable by majority trust vote. No other paid staff.
Anti-capture rules
No politician can be a trust member No funder can sit on the trust Methodology is Creative Commons licensed Any group can use the SpotClean name without permission Platform code is open-source on GitHub All finances publicly visible in real time

The open-source licence is the most important governance mechanism. If the organisation is ever captured or goes dormant, anyone can continue. The movement cannot be killed by killing the organisation.

10
Technology
The digital platform
One lightweight progressive web app — no app store required, works on any smartphone, functional on 2G. Four core functions only. No feature creep.
Function 1 — Spot map

A live public map of all spots

Anyone can submit a spot — one photo, one GPS pin, one sentence. 90 seconds. No signup required.

Nominated Funded Cleaned Maintained Needs attention
Function 2 — Clean reports

Permanent public record for every spot

After cleaning: GPS-verified before photo, GPS-verified after photo, team size, materials used, waste volume, disposal method, and the municipal officer's name. This record never disappears.

Function 3 — Monthly check-ins

What makes the clean permanent

Every cleaned spot has a named local owner. Monthly notification: “How is [spot name] doing?” One photo and a 1–5 rating. If rating drops below 3 for two consecutive months, the spot is flagged and the city node is notified.

Function 4 — Micro-funding

Peer-to-peer, not centralised

Nominated spots can accept donations capped at ₹25,000. All donors named publicly. Funds go directly to the spot team lead's UPI ID against receipts. The platform trust never touches spot-level money.

Technical requirements
Progressive web app — no app store install Works on 2G and 3G networks Offline photo capture, uploads when connected GPS mandatory for all photo submissions Open source on GitHub from day one Target under ₹15,000/month hosting cost
